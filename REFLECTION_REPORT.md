# Reflection Report

Working on *Shift Manager* taught me a lot about scope, feedback, and what actually makes a game fun for other people instead of just interesting in my own head. Going into this project, I had a lot of ideas for systems, upgrades, progression, and story. By the end, I realized that the most important thing was making the game readable, playable, and satisfying from one day to the next. Looking at both the peer reviews and the survey responses, I think the game ended up in a pretty good place, but there are also some clear areas where it could be stronger.

## User Feedback

The survey feedback was encouraging overall. Most of the responses were positive, especially for enjoyment, visuals, and mechanics. That was honestly reassuring because those are the areas that matter most for whether the game is actually working. A few of the written comments stood out to me. Some players said the game was addictive, liked the learning curve, and enjoyed watching their money go up. One person specifically mentioned liking how you get better the more times you play, and another said the tycoon side was fun and addictive. That tells me the core loop is doing what I wanted it to do. The player is not just completing random tasks, but feels like they are improving while also building something over time.

I was also glad to see that people liked the day system, the realism, and the general design of the game. That mattered to me because a big goal of the project was to make the warehouse setting feel like more than just a background for minigames. I wanted it to feel like a whole shift, with pressure building over time, and the home-life systems helped support that. Based on the feedback, I think that part came through.

The most useful criticism had to do with difficulty and pacing. A couple of people liked that the game was stressful and intense, but one comment in particular said that the later part of the game started to feel more tedious than stressful. I think that is fair. Stress is part of the point of the game, but it still has to stay interesting. If the pressure keeps going up without enough new variation, then eventually it can start to feel repetitive instead of exciting. That is probably the biggest thing I would want to fix if I kept working on it, which would be done by adding more minigames and variation to your tasks.

Another thing I noticed from the feedback is that players generally seemed to think the tycoon scaling was fair and that they would recommend the game. That tells me the base concept is solid. The game does not need a complete redesign. It mostly needs refinement, balancing, and more variety in the later sections.

## Peer Feedback

The peer reviews helped a lot, especially because they pointed out issues earlier in development before everything was locked in.

In the first peer review, the main feedback was about structure, progression, and clarity. People wanted the game to feel more complete and easier to follow. They suggested things like a main menu, save functionality, a clearer day flow, more progression, and better text presentation. Looking back, I think this feedback had a big effect on the final version of the project. Adding the main menu, continue system, persistent saves, and a stronger campaign structure made the game feel much more like a full experience instead of just a group of separate mechanics. I also improved the dialogue and briefing layout, which helped the story sections feel less messy.

In the second peer review, the feedback focused more on readability inside the game itself. People wanted better directions for the info and stock screens, clearer environmental details, and more optional interactions like a vending machine speed boost. I did make progress on that by improving the HUD and adding better signposting for the different screens. I think that helped a lot. At the same time, I do not think I fully solved the environment clarity issue. The world could still do a better job of showing players where things are, what they can interact with, and how to interact. That part of the feedback still feels unfinished.

What is interesting is that the survey responses lined up with the peer reviews in a pretty consistent way. The feedback that I acted on the most, like structure and progression, seems to have improved the game in noticeable ways. The feedback that I only partially addressed, like readability and variety, still showed up later as weaker points. That makes the feedback feel valid instead of random. Different players were noticing the same kinds of problems.

## How I Would Improve the Game

If I were improving the game further, the first thing I would work on is late-game variety. I think the current difficulty ramp works in the sense that the game gets harder, but it does not always stay fresh enough. I would want to add more event combinations, more variation between days, and maybe more changes to how certain tasks play out later in the game. That way the player would still feel pressure, but not the same kind of pressure in the same way every time.

The second thing I would improve is the tycoon side of the game. Players seemed to like earning money and seeing progression, so I think that system is worth expanding. Right now it works, but it could be deeper. I would add more upgrade choices, more tradeoffs, and maybe more ways for the player to shape their own strategy. That would make the progression feel less linear and give the player more ownership over how they want to play.

The third area I would improve is onboarding and visual clarity. Even though I added more prompts and HUD information, I still think the game could do a better job of teaching players naturally. Better station markers, more readable walls and doors, clearer interaction cues, and maybe a little more tutorial support would all help. That would especially matter for first-time players.

I also think small optional systems could help the game feel more alive. One example from peer feedback was adding something like a vending machine energy drink speed-boost. I did not get to that, but I still think it is a good idea. Small choices like that could break up the routine, add personality, and make the stressful parts feel more manageable without removing the challenge.

## Future Work

If I kept working on this project, I would probably focus on four main things: more minigame variety, a deeper upgrade system, clearer environmental design, and more polish overall.

More minigame variety would help solve the biggest pacing issue. A deeper upgrade system would make the tycoon side more meaningful. Clearer environmental design would make the game easier to understand without relying so heavily on text prompts. More polish would include better visual feedback, stronger animation, more sound effects, and just making the whole game feel smoother and more finished.

I would also like to explore adding more character and story interactions. The home-life side of the game added a lot to the tone, and I think building that out more could make the game feel even more personal and memorable.

## Conclusion

Overall, I feel good about how the project turned out. The feedback suggests that the core of the game works. People generally found it fun, engaging, and interesting to improve at over time. At the same time, the feedback also made it clear that the game still needs more variety, more visual clarity, and a little more polish in the later sections. If I kept developing it, I would not start over. I would build on what is already working and focus on making the experience feel more consistent from beginning to end.
